A word on free hosting

Free tiers are genuinely fine for many small business sites, and I'll tell you when yours is one of them rather than sell you hosting you don't need. The one real catch: most free tiers put the site to sleep when nobody's visiting, so the first person to arrive after a quiet spell can wait twenty seconds or more for a page. If people find you through search rather than knowing your name already, that's worth paying a few pounds a month to avoid.

// Questions about money

The things people ask before they ask for a quote.

Why is there a range rather than one price?

Because a five-page site for a plumber and a five-page site with a booking system are different jobs. The range tells you roughly where you'll land; you get one fixed number before any work starts, and that number doesn't move unless you ask for something new.

Do you take a deposit?

Yes — 50% before work begins, the balance when the site goes live. For larger applications I split it across phases so you're never far ahead of what's been delivered.

What happens if I want changes later?

Two rounds of changes are included while we're building. After launch, small text edits are covered by a care plan, and anything bigger is quoted at my day rate before I start.

Do I own the website?

Yes. The code and the copyright transfer to you on final payment. There's no licence to keep paying and nothing that stops you taking it to another developer.

Are you VAT registered?

Not currently, so there's no VAT to add to these prices. If that changes I'll say so before quoting.

Why are you cheaper than an agency?

There's one of me and no office. You get the person who writes the code rather than an account manager relaying messages to a junior. The trade-off is honest: an agency can put five people on a deadline and I can't, so for very large or very urgent projects they may genuinely suit you better.

Tell me what you need and I'll send back a fixed price and a timeline — usually within one working day.
